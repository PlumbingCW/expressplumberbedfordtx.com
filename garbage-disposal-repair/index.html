Emergency garbage disposal repair in Bedford, TX covers units that hum without turning, trip the breaker, leak from the sides or bottom, or let the sink back up. Disposals live a hard life, and the typical failure is a jam, a worn seal or a motor that has given out.

We also install new disposals. A new unit mounts in the same sink flange in most homes, and a replacement visit often takes about an hour once the plumber arrives with the right model.

Disposal failure: what to do first

If the disposal is leaking, humming or smoking, stop using it right away. Never put your hand in the unit, even if it seems off.

Do these steps.

Switch off the power

Turn off the wall switch and flip the breaker for the disposal if you can find it.

Never reach inside

Use tongs or pliers to remove a visible object after the power is off. Keep hands out of the unit.

Stop running the sink

If the sink is backed up, do not add water. Use a bucket under any leak.

Check the reset button

Many disposals have a red reset button on the bottom. Press it once after the unit cools.

What goes wrong with disposals

Hum but no spin

The flywheel is jammed. A hex wrench in the bottom socket can free it, once the power is off.

Leak from the top

The sink flange seal or the mounting has failed.

Leak from the side or bottom

A cracked housing or worn internal seal usually means replacing the unit.

Sink drains slowly after use

A clogged drain line or trap, not just the disposal.

Repair or replace

Jams and small clogs are usually quick repairs. A leaking housing or a burned-out motor means replacement. Most disposals last roughly 8 to 12 years, and a unit that is older and starting to fail is rarely worth fixing more than once.

Grease is the quiet enemy. It does not grind away. It coats the drain line behind the disposal and narrows it until the sink backs up. We explain what to keep out of the disposal and when the drain line itself needs attention.

Repair when

  • The unit is jammed but sound.
  • The reset button restores power.
  • The leak is at a drain connection.
  • The unit is relatively new.

Replace when

  • The housing leaks.
  • The motor is dead or burned out.
  • The unit is old and failing.
  • You want a quieter or stronger model.

Kitchen drain lines

Grease and food waste build up behind the disposal. Cleaning the line may be needed even after the unit is fixed.

Septic and sewer

Bedford homes are on city sewer. Heavy food waste still adds load to the line, so go easy on fibrous scraps.

Sink flange

If you replace a sink or countertop, the disposal flange and the mounting need attention.

Dishwasher connection

The dishwasher drain connects to the disposal in many homes, so a clog there can cause water in the dishwasher.
