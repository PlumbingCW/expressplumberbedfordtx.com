A sewer camera inspection in Bedford, TX sends a small waterproof camera through the clean-out and along the line toward the street. The picture shows cracks, joints that have separated, roots, grease, scale and sags that hold water. It replaces guesswork with a picture you can look at yourself.

This is planned work. If sewage is already backing up, call about emergency sewer line repair instead. For buyers, owners with repeat clogs and anyone planning a remodel, an inspection is an inexpensive way to learn the line condition before money is spent.

When an inspection makes sense

Buying an older home

Houses from the growth years after 1960 can have original clay or cast iron laterals. A pre-purchase camera pass reveals problems before closing.

Clogs that keep returning

Repeated stoppages often have a physical cause such as a sag, a crack or roots.

Before a remodel

Adding a bathroom or a slab-level fixture loads the line. Check it first.

After a repair

A follow-up pass confirms that a lining or replacement is clean and flowing.

What the camera finds

Roots show up as fine hairs at joints or thick masses that fill the pipe. Cracks appear as lines or missing sections of pipe wall. A belly, sometimes called a sag, shows as standing water in a low spot. Offsets are joints where one pipe has shifted away from the next. Scale and grease look like rough buildup on the pipe wall.

Many cameras also include a locator transmitter. It lets the plumber mark the depth and path of the pipe on the surface, which helps plan a repair without guessing where to dig.

Home buyers

Bedford has many established neighborhoods. A camera pass on the lateral is one of the few ways to see the condition of buried pipe before closing.

Large trees

Older lots often have mature trees. The camera shows whether their roots have entered the line.

Clean-out access

A clean-out outside the house makes the inspection quick. If there is none, a toilet may need to be pulled to reach the line.

Limits of the camera

A camera sees the inside of the pipe. It does not measure soil conditions or replace a foundation inspection.
