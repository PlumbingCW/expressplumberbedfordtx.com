Sewer Camera Inspection Before Buying in Bedford, TX

A standard home inspection does not look inside the sewer line. A camera does. For an older Bedford house, a sewer scope before closing can reveal a problem you would otherwise inherit.

By the Bedford Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-14

Plumber on call, 24/7.(817) 601-6266Call Now

A sewer camera inspection before buying a home in Bedford, TX is worth doing for any house with an older lateral, large trees nearby or a history of drain trouble. A standard home inspection looks at fixtures and visible plumbing. It does not look inside the buried line between the house and the street, where cracks, roots, sags and offset joints hide. A short camera visit shows them on video.

The result can change your negotiation. A clean line is peace in a file. A damaged line gives you a documented reason to ask for a repair credit, a seller repair or a price change.

What the camera can find

Roots

Fine roots at joints or masses that fill the pipe.

Cracks and breaks

Lines or missing sections in the pipe wall.

Bellies and sags

Low spots that hold water and solids.

Offset joints

Sections that have shifted out of line.

The camera also shows the pipe material. Clay tile, cast iron and plastic age in different ways. Older cast iron can scale and rust. Clay can crack or let roots in at the joints. Plastic is more resistant but can sag if the trench was not bedded well.

Why older Bedford homes deserve a closer look

Bedford has grown from a small town to a city of around 50,000, and much of the housing came during that growth. A house built decades ago may still have its original lateral. That is not automatically a problem. It is a reason to look instead of guess.

How the process works

Get access

Ask the seller to allow a visit, and find the clean-out. If there is no clean-out, a toilet may need to be pulled.

Run the camera

The plumber sends it along the line and records the video.

Mark the line

A locator can mark depth and path if you need a repair estimate.

Review the footage

The plumber explains what the video shows and what it means.

Ask for a copy of the video and any notes. Share them with your agent and your home inspector. If a defect shows up, a plumber can also give a repair plan to support a negotiation.

Questions to ask the city before closing

Ask the City of Bedford which department handles sewer service and whether there are known issues in the street. The City's Water Billing page covers utility billing for water, sewer and solid waste, so your account will be tied to the property.

Ask building inspections about any past permits for plumbing work at the address. The answers help you understand what has been changed and when.

Limits of a camera

A camera sees the inside of the pipe. It does not show soil conditions, and it cannot see past a full blockage. If the line is blocked, it needs cleaning first. A camera also cannot tell you how long a pipe will last. It tells you what condition it is in today.

Timing the inspection inside the option period

In a typical Texas purchase, the buyer has a short option period to inspect the home and negotiate. A sewer scope should be scheduled early in that window so there is time to get a repair quote and discuss the result. Ask your agent how long your option period is and book the camera visit as soon as it starts.

If the clean-out is hidden or missing, tell the plumber before the visit. They may need to pull a toilet, which adds time and needs the seller's permission.

Keep the video for your own records after closing. It is a baseline you can compare with a later inspection if the line gives trouble.

If you are the seller

Sellers can benefit from a scope too. A clean video before listing answers a buyer's question before it is asked. If the scope finds a problem, you can repair it on your schedule, with your plumber and without the pressure of an option period.

Disclose what you know. A recorded repair with a receipt is much easier to explain to a buyer than a surprise discovered late in the process.

Ask your plumber to provide the video and a short written summary you can share with the listing agent.

What a clean scope does and does not promise

A clean video tells you the pipe was in good shape on the day of the visit. It does not guarantee how long it will last, and it does not cover the city main or parts of the line the camera could not reach. Roots grow, soil moves and pipes age. A clean scope is a good baseline, not a lifetime warranty.

If the camera could not pass a section, ask why. A bend, a belly full of water or a collapse can stop the camera. Each of these is useful information, and each can be followed up with another visit after cleaning.

Plan to scope again if you see new signs after you move in, such as repeat clogs or a wet patch in the yard.
