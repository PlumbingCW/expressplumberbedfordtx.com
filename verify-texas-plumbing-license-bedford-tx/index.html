How to Check a Plumbing License in Bedford, TX

Texas licenses plumbers through the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. You can look any plumber up in minutes. Here are the steps and the questions worth asking.

By the Bedford Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-14

Plumber on call, 24/7.(817) 601-6266Call Now

To check a plumber's license in Bedford, Texas, use the public license search on the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners website. Search by name or license number, pick the plumbing license type and read the record. It shows the status, the expiration date and any disciplinary actions. Ask any plumber for a license number first. A legitimate one gives it to you.

The check takes a few minutes and protects you from unlicensed work. It matters most for jobs that involve gas, water heaters, sewer lines and anything that needs a permit.

Steps to look up a license

Open the search

Go to the TSBPE website and choose the option to find a license or registration.

Pick a search type

Search by name, or by license number if the plumber gave you one.

Choose the board and license type

Select the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners and the plumbing licensed or registered category.

Open the record

Click the plumber's name and review the license page.

The license page shows the license status, the expiration date, fingerprint status and any disciplinary actions. If a field is blank, the board has no record for that category. Several names may appear in the results, because some license numbers are shared across license types. That is normal.

What to look for

Active status

A license that has expired or been suspended is a reason to walk away.

Matching name

The name on the record should match the person and the company you are dealing with.

License type

Master plumbers, journeyman plumbers and apprentices have different roles and limits.

Disciplinary history

Read any actions listed and decide whether they concern you.

Questions to ask any plumber

Before you hire

  • What is your license number and who holds it?
  • Do you carry liability insurance?
  • Will you give me a written price before work starts?
  • Does this job need a permit and who pulls it?
  • What is covered if something goes wrong after the visit?

We are not asking you to take our word for it. Ask us for the license number and look it up like any other company. A plumber who is uncomfortable with that is not the one to hire.

Permits in Bedford

Many plumbing jobs need a permit, and the City of Bedford building inspections office can tell you whether yours does. Ask before work starts, not after.

The city's website lists its departments and contact options. If your plumber says a permit is not needed, ask them to confirm with the city.

Understanding license types

Texas has several levels of plumbing licensing. A master plumber can supervise others and pull permits. A journeyman works under a master. An apprentice works under supervision while training. A company that sends an unsupervised apprentice to your house is a concern, and a quick question on the phone can sort that out.

Ask who will be at your home and what license they hold. A company can be legitimate and still send a crew with different license levels. What matters is that the work is supervised and done correctly.

Estimates, deposits and paperwork

For a larger job, ask for a written scope, the price and any deposit terms before work begins. A reasonable deposit for materials is common on bigger projects. A demand for the full amount in cash before any work is a red flag.

After the work, ask for a receipt that lists what was done, the parts used and any warranty. Keep it with the permit paperwork, if one was needed. These records matter when you sell the house or file an insurance claim.

What to do if something goes wrong

Start with the company. Describe the problem in writing, attach photos and ask for a fix within a reasonable time. Most legitimate companies want to correct their work. Keep copies of everything you send.

If the company does not respond, the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners accepts complaints about licensed plumbers and can also act when someone works without a license. Check the board's website for how to file. Your city building inspections office can also help if the work was permitted and failed inspection.

Do not withhold payment without reading your contract and talking to the company first. A calm paper trail serves you better than a heated phone call.

A five-minute routine before any visit

Write down the company name, the technician's name and the license number the company gives you. Look up the number while the plumber is on the way. If the name on the record does not match the person who arrives, ask why before they start.

Take a photo of the vehicle and any paperwork you sign. If something goes wrong, those records help the company, the board and your insurer understand what happened. It is a small habit that costs nothing and pays off rarely but sharply.

Do the same for every trade you hire. Electricians, roofers and HVAC contractors are licensed too, and the lookups work in a similar way.
