Repiping in Bedford, TX replaces the water supply lines inside a house. It is a planned project that makes sense when pipes leak repeatedly, when water runs brown or when pressure has dropped because corrosion has narrowed the lines. It is not an emergency repair, though a burst pipe can start the conversation.

Bedford grew from about 2,700 residents in 1960 to around 50,000 today, so many homes date from the decades when galvanized steel, copper and, for a period, polybutylene plastic were common. Each material ages differently, and the one in your walls shapes the decision.

Signs your pipes may need replacing

Repeated leaks

Patching one pinhole leak and finding another within months suggests the whole run is wearing out.

Rusty or discolored water

Brown water from galvanized pipe means the inside is corroding.

Falling water pressure

Mineral and rust buildup narrows old pipes until flow drops.

Polybutylene pipe

Gray plastic pipe used in some homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s has a history of failures. A plumber can identify it.

Choosing the right pipe

PEX is flexible, resists corrosion and can often be threaded through walls with fewer openings. Copper is durable and familiar, with a longer record in homes. The right choice depends on water chemistry, budget and how the house is built.

The City of Bedford's 2025 water quality report lists a pH of 8.8 and chloramine as the disinfectant. We do not claim a pipe material is immune to any water. We recommend a material based on the house and explain the tradeoffs.

PEX

  • Flexible and quicker to install.
  • Resists corrosion and scale.
  • Fewer wall openings in many homes.
  • Must be protected from sunlight.

Copper

  • Long track record in homes.
  • Handles heat and fire well.
  • Takes more labor to install.
  • Can develop pinholes in some conditions.

How a repipe works

Inspect

We check pipe type, access, pressure and fixture count.

Plan

You get a written price and a plan for the order of work.

Replace

New lines run to each fixture, with new shutoffs where needed.

Test and patch

We pressure-test, restore water and patch the access openings.

A whole-home repipe usually takes a few days, depending on the size of the house. The plumber explains the timeline and what to expect for water shutoffs and wall patching. Some homeowners choose to repipe in stages, replacing the worst zones first.

What moves the price

Standards and codes behind the work

Supply lines are sized and installed under the plumbing code, and a repipe usually needs a permit and inspection. PEX and copper both carry standards for potable water use.
