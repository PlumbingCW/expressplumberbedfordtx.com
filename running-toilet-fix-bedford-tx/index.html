Running Toilet in Bedford, TX: Find the Cause

A toilet that runs on its own wastes water and raises your bill. A dye test finds the leak and a flapper or fill valve usually fixes it.

By the Bedford Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-14

Plumber on call, 24/7.(817) 601-6266Call Now

A toilet that runs is almost always leaking from the tank into the bowl or overfilling. The usual causes are a worn flapper that does not seal, a fill valve that does not shut off or a chain that is too tight or too loose. A dye test in the tank shows whether the flapper is leaking. Replacing the flapper or fill valve is inexpensive and takes a short visit.

The leak is silent. You may notice the sound of refilling every few minutes, or you may notice only a rising bill. A toilet that leaks even slowly can waste many gallons a day.

The dye test

Add dye

Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank, not the bowl.

Wait

Do not flush for fifteen minutes.

Look at the bowl

If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

Flush and clean

Flush to clear the dye so it does not stain.

The usual culprits

Worn flapper

The rubber hardens or warps and no longer seals.

Fill valve that will not shut off

Water keeps flowing into the overflow tube.

Chain trouble

A chain that is too short holds the flapper open, and a chain that is too long tangles.

Float set too high

Water spills into the overflow tube instead of stopping.

Mineral buildup can also keep a flapper from seating. A gentle cleaning of the seat sometimes fixes a leak without any new parts.

When it is more than a part

A cracked tank, a leaking tank bolt or a leak at the base are different problems. A leak at the base can be a failed wax ring, and it can rot the floor. Any water around the toilet base deserves attention. If the toilet also rocks, the flange may be damaged.

Water and bills in Bedford

The City of Bedford's 2025 Water Quality Report lists total hardness of 110 ppm. Moderately hard water leaves mineral deposits that can foul flappers and fill valves over time.

The City's Water Billing page links an EyeOnWater sign-up for tracking usage. If your bill jumps and the toilet is the suspect, a usage graph can show whether the leak is constant.

What it costs to ignore it

A toilet leak adds to your bill every day. The parts cost little compared with the water wasted over a few months. If you replace the flapper and the problem returns, the fill valve or the seat is the next suspect.

Replacing a flapper step by step

Shut off the water

Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise and flush to empty the tank.

Remove the old flapper

Unhook it from the pegs on the overflow tube and from the chain.

Match the new one

Bring the old flapper to the store so the size and style match.

Set the chain

Leave a little slack, not so much that it can tangle under the flapper.

Turn the water back on and watch the tank refill. Flush a couple of times and check that the flapper seals. Repeat the dye test to confirm the leak is gone.

Replacing the fill valve

If the toilet hisses or water keeps flowing into the overflow tube, the fill valve is the likely culprit. Replacement kits are inexpensive and universal in size. Shut off the water, disconnect the supply line, remove the old valve through the bottom of the tank and install the new one at the same height.

Adjust the float so that the water stops about an inch below the top of the overflow tube. If the tank still overfills, the new valve may be faulty or the seat may be dirty.

If you are not comfortable working under a toilet tank, a plumber can do both repairs in a single short visit.

Signs the problem is not the tank

If the toilet runs only after you flush and the bowl level drops, the trap or the siphon jet may be the issue. If you see water trickling down the inside of the bowl without any flush, the flapper is the usual cause. If the water level in the bowl rises and falls on its own, a partial blockage in the drain can pull and push water.

Listen to the pattern. A hiss that never stops points to the fill valve. A refill every few minutes points to the flapper. A gurgle in other drains points to a venting or drain problem.

A plumber can sort these out in one visit. Describing the sound is often enough to save a trip to the store.

Water savings and why they matter

A toilet that runs even a little adds to every bill. Over a few months the waste can exceed the cost of a new flapper by a wide margin. Fixing the leak is one of the simplest ways to cut a household's water use without changing any habits.

When you replace parts, choose ones that match your tank brand. Universal parts fit many tanks, but a mismatched flapper may not seal. If you are unsure, take a photo of the inside of the tank and show it at the store.

If the same toilet runs again within weeks, stop buying parts and call a plumber. A cracked overflow tube or a pitted seat will not respond to a new flapper.
