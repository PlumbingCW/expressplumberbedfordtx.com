Slab leak repair in Bedford, TX deals with a leak in a water line that runs under or through the concrete foundation. Many houses in North Texas are built on slab foundations, so the supply pipes sometimes run below or inside the concrete, where a leak cannot be seen.

Slab leaks are urgent because water under a foundation can undermine soil and run up the bill fast. They are also tricky, because the repair choice depends on exactly where the pipe has failed and what the rest of the line looks like.

Suspected slab leak: what to do before we arrive

If you hear running water with everything off, feel a hot spot on the floor or see the meter moving, close the main valve and call the 24/7 line. The longer water flows, the more soil and flooring it can affect.

Do these in order.

Close the main valve

Shut off water to the house at the main. If the meter stops, the leak is on the house side.

Turn off the water heater

If hot water lines are suspected, switch off the heater to avoid running it dry.

Check floors and baseboards

Look for damp carpet, lifted flooring or hot spots. Mark what you find with tape.

Note the meter reading

Photograph the meter before and after an hour with water off. It helps the plumber and any insurance claim.

Signs of a slab leak

Warm or hot spots on the floor

A leaking hot line can heat the concrete above it.

Sound of running water

A hiss or trickle when every fixture is off is a classic sign.

Unexplained high water bills

A steady climb with no change in habits points to a leak somewhere.

Damp floor, cracks or musty smell

Moisture under flooring can lift planks, stain carpet and encourage mold.

Finding the leak before cutting concrete

Plumbers combine listening devices, pressure tests and thermal imaging to narrow the leak to a small area. Isolating hot and cold lines shows which side is failing. Good location work means a smaller opening in the floor and less repair afterward.

Once located, there are three common paths. A spot repair opens the slab at the leak and replaces that section. A reroute abandons the damaged line and runs new pipe through walls or the attic. Repiping replaces the lines more broadly when several have failed.

Slab construction

A large share of Texas homes sit on slab-on-grade foundations, so water lines under the floor are common in Bedford housing.

Clay soil movement

North Texas clay swells and shrinks with moisture. Foundation shift can stress buried copper lines and joints.

Water chemistry

The City of Bedford's 2025 water quality report lists a pH of 8.8 and chloramine disinfection. Pipe wear depends on many factors, so ask a plumber about the age and material of your lines.

Older copper

Homes with original copper from earlier decades can develop pinhole leaks along a line.

Standards and codes behind the work

Repairs through a slab are done to the plumbing code, with pressure testing before the concrete is closed. A structural engineer should assess any visible foundation damage, because that is outside plumbing.
