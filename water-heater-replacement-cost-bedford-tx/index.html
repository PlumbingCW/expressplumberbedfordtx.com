Water Heater Replacement Cost in Bedford, TX

The price of a new water heater in Bedford depends on five things: the unit, the fuel, the venting, the space and the hour of the call. Here is how each one moves the total.

By the Bedford Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-14

Plumber on call, 24/7.(817) 601-6266Call Now

The cost of replacing a water heater in Bedford, TX depends mainly on five things. They are the size and type of the new unit, whether you keep the same fuel, how much venting or wiring changes, how hard the space is to work in and when you call. A like-for-like swap in an open garage sits at the low end. A fuel change or a tankless upgrade sits much higher.

We do not print dollar figures here because they go stale quickly and vary by brand, model and the condition of your home. What we can do is show you what each part of the quote means, so two estimates can be compared on the same terms.

The unit itself

Tank heaters are sold by capacity and by efficiency rating. A bigger tank or a higher-efficiency model costs more to buy. Heat pump hybrids and tankless units cost more still. The first-hour rating on the label tells you how much hot water the unit can deliver in a busy hour, and that is the number to match to your household.

A plumber who recommends the largest tank on the truck is not sizing your home. Ask how many people, bathrooms and hot water appliances the recommendation assumes. A household of two does not need the same unit as a family of six.

Fuel and venting

Keeping the same fuel is the cheapest path. Gas units reuse the gas line and the flue. Electric units reuse the circuit. Changing from gas to electric, or from tank to tankless, can mean a new vent, a bigger gas line or a new heavy circuit. Each of those adds labor and materials.

Venting is the line item people miss. A gas tank needs a proper flue that draws well. A condensing tankless unit needs a condensate drain. If the existing vent is corroded or undersized, replacing it is a safety item, not an upsell.

Like-for-like swap

  • Same fuel and same size.
  • Existing vent and wiring reused.
  • Often done in one visit.
  • Lowest total cost.

Upgrade or fuel change

  • New vent, gas line or circuit.
  • May need a permit and inspection.
  • Longer install visit.
  • Higher total cost and sometimes lower running cost.

The space and the code work

A water heater in an open garage is easy to reach. One in an attic, a tight closet or a utility room with little clearance takes longer and can need extra safety parts. A drain pan and a piped discharge line from the relief valve are standard in finished spaces. An expansion tank is common where the system is closed.

Permit rules differ by job. Ask City of Bedford building inspections whether yours needs one. If it does, the plumber should explain the steps and what the permit adds to the schedule.

The hour of the call

An emergency replacement at midnight costs more than a scheduled one on a weekday afternoon, because dispatch outside normal hours is more expensive to staff. If the tank is only old and not leaking, a planned install usually costs less and lets you pick the unit. If it is leaking, shut the cold inlet and decide how fast you need hot water back.

How to compare two quotes

What to do before you sign a quote

  • Is the quote for the same fuel type and the same capacity?
  • Does the price include venting, a new relief valve and haul-away?
  • Who pulls the permit if one is needed?
  • What does the manufacturer warranty cover, and what workmanship coverage is offered?
  • Is the price written down before work starts?

If one quote is far lower than the others, look for the missing line. A cheaper number sometimes leaves out venting, a pan or the permit. A higher quote sometimes includes a larger unit than the house needs.

Bedford water and the life of a new tank

The City of Bedford's 2025 Water Quality Report lists total hardness of 110 ppm as calcium carbonate. That is about 6.4 grains per gallon, our conversion, and falls in the moderately hard range on the USGS scale. Scale settles in the bottom of a tank over time.

The same report lists chloramine as the disinfectant and a pH of 8.8. Rubber seals and anode rods wear in different ways in different water, so ask your plumber about flushing and anode checks on a new tank. A yearly flush is cheap protection.

Source: City of Bedford 2025 Water Quality Report, USGS hardness scale, grains per gallon is our conversion.

When replacement beats repair

A leak through the tank shell, rust-colored hot water or a unit past roughly ten years old usually points to replacement. A failed element or thermostat on a younger tank usually points to repair. Our water heater repair page explains how we decide.

Warranty terms that change the real cost

A manufacturer warranty usually covers the tank or the heat exchanger for a stated number of years and covers parts for a shorter period. Labor is often not included. A longer tank warranty can add to the price of the unit, so ask whether the extra years are worth it for how long you plan to stay in the house.

Ask the plumber what workmanship coverage applies to the installation itself, and get the answer in writing. A leak at a fitting two months after the install is a workmanship question, not a manufacturer one.
