Tankless Water Heater in Bedford, TX

A tankless water heater in Bedford, TX heats water as it flows instead of storing it, so the hot water does not run out the way a tank can. The trade-off is more demanding installation and regular care, because the heat exchanger works hard and mineral scale builds on it.

Service calls for tankless units follow a pattern. Some people want a new install, some want a flush because the unit is throwing an error code, and some want to understand the warranty before they buy. This page covers all three.

Flush and descale: the maintenance that matters

Scale is the most common tankless problem. Bedford's water is moderately hard, with total hardness of 110 ppm in the City of Bedford's 2025 water quality report. That is not the hardest water in Texas, but a heat exchanger runs hot enough for minerals to settle on it over months.

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit using a small pump and hoses, then rinses it clean. Many manufacturers tie warranty coverage to regular flushing, so keep the service record. We note the date and the water condition after each flush.

Error codes

Codes for ignition, flame, flow or temperature often trace to scale, a dirty inlet screen or a venting issue.

Temperature swings

Hot water that surges between hot and cool suggests a flow problem or scale on the exchanger.

Longer waits

If hot water takes longer than it used to, a blocked filter or scaled coil may be limiting flow.

Noise changes

Kettling or rumbling during heating is another scale clue.

Installing a tankless unit

Gas tankless units often need a larger gas line than the tank they replace, plus stainless or approved venting. Electric tankless units need heavy circuits that some panels cannot support. Both need a condensate path in condensing models and a way to isolate the unit for flushing.

We confirm the gas supply size, the vent route and the panel capacity before you commit. If the house is not a good candidate, we say so and explain what a tank or hybrid would offer instead.

Tankless advantages

  • Hot water does not run out of a stored supply.
  • Compact wall-mounted unit.
  • Longer expected service life when maintained.
  • Lower standby heat loss.

Tankless trade-offs

  • Higher installed cost.
  • Larger gas line or heavy electrical load.
  • Needs yearly flushing in moderately hard water.
  • Very small flow cannot always trigger the burner.

Standards and codes behind the work

Gas tankless heaters are certified to ANSI Z21.10.3, and the maker's flush and venting instructions are part of the warranty. We follow them and note the service date.
