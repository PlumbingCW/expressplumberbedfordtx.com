Water line repair in Bedford, TX covers the buried pipe that brings water from the city meter into the house. This line is the homeowner's responsibility from the meter onward. When it cracks, corrodes or gets cut, the symptoms can be loud or quiet.

A major break floods a yard and drops pressure to a trickle. A slow leak shows up as a bill that climbs, a damp strip of lawn or a meter that turns when every fixture is off. Either one needs attention, because buried leaks wash out soil and waste water continuously.

Broken water line: what to do first

If water is surfacing in the yard or you have lost pressure suddenly, shut the house side of the water supply and call the 24/7 line.

Here is what to do.

Close the main shutoff

Use the valve at the house, or the meter valve if you can reach it safely.

Watch the meter

If the dial keeps moving after the house valve is closed, the leak is between the meter and the valve.

Mark the wet area

Flag the damp or sunken ground so the plumber can see it quickly.

Keep digging off the list

Do not dig with a shovel near the line. You may hit gas, electric or communication lines.

Signs the service line is failing

Low pressure everywhere

When all fixtures drop at once, the problem may be in the main line or at the meter.

Wet or sunken yard

Water surfacing between the meter and the house marks a buried break.

Meter spins with everything off

The clearest test of a leak between the meter and the house.

Discolored or air-filled water

Pipe damage can let air and dirt into the line.

Repair or replace

A single cut or a failed fitting can be repaired by digging at that spot. A line that is old, corroded or leaking in several places is usually better replaced. Replacement can be done by trenching, or by trenchless methods such as pipe bursting where the soil and access allow.

Before digging we call for utility locates so we do not hit gas, electric or other buried lines. That step is normal practice and sometimes adds a short wait.

Meter responsibility

The City of Bedford owns the meter. The line from the meter to the house is typically the owner's. Confirm the dividing point with the city utility billing office.

Monitoring usage

Bedford utility billing offers online tools for tracking usage. A sudden jump with no change in habits is worth a leak check.

Pipe materials

Older lines may be copper, galvanized or early plastic. The material affects how long it lasts and how it is repaired.

Pressure regulator

Homes often have a pressure reducing valve. A failed one can cause both high and low pressure symptoms.
