Leak detection in Bedford, TX is the process of locating a water leak you cannot see, such as a pipe in a wall, under a slab, behind a ceiling or in the yard between the meter and the house. The goal is to pinpoint the break so repairs open a small area, not a whole room.

Most hidden leaks announce themselves indirectly. The water bill rises, the sound of running water continues when everything is off, a floor feels warm, or a ceiling stain spreads. Callers describe the symptom, not the word leak, and that is where the search begins.

Active leak: stop the water first

If water is flowing into a room, treat it as an emergency and stop the supply before anything else. Then call, because hidden leaks do their damage out of sight.

Here is what to do while you wait.

Close the main valve

Turn the main shutoff to the house off. Know where it is before you need it.

Kill power in wet areas

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off the breaker for that area. Do not stand in water to do it.

Move belongings

Lift items from wet floors and shift furniture away from damp walls.

Photograph for insurance

Take dated photos of stains, damage and the meter reading.

Start with the meter test

The simplest check costs nothing. Turn off every tap and appliance, then watch the water meter. If the dial or low-flow indicator moves, water is going somewhere. The City of Bedford's utility billing page also links an EyeOnWater sign-up for tracking usage, which can show a leak pattern over days.

If the meter holds still when the house is off, the leak may be on the hot side, in the irrigation system or only under pressure when a fixture runs. A plumber narrows it with isolation tests.

High bill, no change in habits

Usage that climbs month after month without more people or watering points to a leak.

Meter moves with water off

This is the clearest sign of a leak between the meter and the fixtures.

Warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking under a slab can warm the floor above it.

Stains or mold on a ceiling or wall

Water behind drywall tends to travel before it shows.

How a plumber finds the leak

Tools do the work. Acoustic listening devices hear the sound of water escaping a pressurized pipe. Pressure tests isolate sections of the line. Thermal imaging shows temperature differences behind surfaces, and moisture meters measure dampness in materials. A camera can inspect drain lines when the leak is on the waste side.

We combine these methods because each has limits. A leak under a thick slab may need acoustic and pressure testing together before anyone cuts concrete.

Special cases worth knowing

Ceiling and wall leaks

Water can travel along framing before it shows. The stain is rarely directly below the source.

Under-house and crawlspace

Pier-and-beam sections, where they exist, let a plumber check lines from below.

Irrigation leaks

Texas lawns lean on sprinkler systems. A broken head or valve can leak all day and look like a meter problem.

Buried service line

Water that appears in the yard or around the meter box may come from the line between the meter and the house.

Standards and codes behind the work

Non-invasive tools such as acoustic listening, pressure testing and thermal imaging are standard practice for locating hidden leaks. The method used is chosen for the pipe material and the location.
