Plumbing installation in Bedford, TX covers new fixtures and supply and drain work for remodels and additions. Typical jobs include sinks, faucets, toilets, showers, tubs, dishwashers, washing machine hookups, outdoor spigots and new water lines to a garage or addition.

The work is mostly planned, so you can pick the fixtures, schedule the visit and ask about permits ahead of time. If something is leaking or flooding right now, the repair pages are the faster route, starting with burst pipe repair.

What we install

Sinks and faucets

Kitchen, bath and utility sinks, with new supply lines and shutoff valves.

Toilets and bidets

New or replacement toilets, with flange and wax ring work as needed.

Showers and tubs

Valves, trim and drain connections for new or remodeled baths.

Appliance hookups

Dishwashers, washing machines, ice makers and water heaters.

Planning a remodel

Plumbing changes are easiest before walls are closed. Moving a sink or adding a bathroom means running drains with slope, adding vents and tying into supply lines. We walk through the layout with you and flag anything that will add cost before it surprises you.

Texas plumbing work is regulated by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners, and installations are inspected under the local code. You can check any plumber's license on the TSBPE website, and the City of Bedford building inspections office can tell you whether your project needs a permit.

Older homes

Houses from the post-1960 growth years may have older supply and drain lines. A plumber checks what the new fixture will tie into.

Slab construction

Adding a fixture on a slab can mean cutting concrete for drains, so plan locations carefully.

Water quality

Bedford water is moderately hard at 110 ppm total hardness in the city report, so choose fixtures with finishes that handle scale.

Permits

Ask City of Bedford building inspections what your project needs. We help with the paperwork.

What decides the price of an installation

What moves the price

Fixtures are the visible cost, but the pipe work behind them often decides the price. A written price comes first.
